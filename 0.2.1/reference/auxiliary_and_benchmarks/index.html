    Auxiliary & Benchmark Appendix (Tier 3 & Tier 4)¶

    Not part of the primary product core

    The 23 capabilities catalogued on this page — 11 Tier 3 secondary utilities and 12 Tier 4 internal QA / regression harnesses — are not part of the primary Tier 1 / Tier 2 customer product core. They are documented here only so the inventory stays honest: they are optional utilities or internal QA / regression proof harnesses.

    Why this appendix exists¶

    The customer-facing narrative lives in the documentation home (Tier 1 and Tier 2) and in the Verity Pro Enterprise Features page. Everything a customer is expected to install, run, and depend on day to day is described there.

    This appendix covers the remaining surface area, split into two groups:

    • Tier 3 — optional utilities. Secondary helpers that are useful in narrow situations (schema linting, SBOM export, community threat feeds) but must never be pitched as the core security engine.
    • Tier 4 — internal QA / regression harnesses. Fixtures, mocks, synthetic oracles, and legacy interactive shells whose real job is to prove the engine works, not to serve customers directly.

    Scores are inventory ranks, not product promises

    A high score means the capability is worth keeping somewhere. It does not mean the capability belongs in the primary product story.

    Tier 3 optional-utilities guidance¶

    Tier 3 helpers are shipped, supported, and documented, but they are not the reason a customer adopts Verity. Use them when they fit:

    • Schema linting. The readiness linter is a design-time aid. Run it on a new MCP server to catch weak tool schemas before release.
    • Compliance export. The CycloneDX and SPDX exporters turn an existing scan result into a secondary SBOM artefact.
    • Community feeds. The notifier and static portal exist to publish a public threat feed, not to scan customer environments.
    • Optional extras. Semantic search stays behind the optional [semantic] install extra and never becomes a hard dependency.

    Three Tier 3 entries are deliberately de-emphasised. The binary scanner depends on an external VirusTotal key or custom blocklist, the container scanner needs a local Trivy binary, and the port-monitoring daemon relies on Unix-only os.fork while performing a noisy 5,801-port sweep. They remain in the code base for internal and power-user use, but the primary CLI surface never advertises them. The typosquat detector is folded quietly into verity scan rather than shipped as a separate command.

    The internal-only helpers — the Trivy wrapper, the ephemeral DuckDB / SQLite engine, and the deep zero-tool repo auditor — stay outside the primary user documentation because they either require external tooling or exist solely to seed the fingerprint catalog.

    Tier 4 internal QA & regression harness boundaries¶

    Tier 4 capabilities are proof infrastructure. They exist so the engine can be regression-tested deterministically, and they deliberately carry boundaries that make them unsuitable as customer features:

    1. Fixtures over inputs. The synthetic oracles, Docker lab, and in-memory cloud mocks respond to fixed JSON flags. They validate the scanner; they do not scan anything real.
    2. Hardcoded payloads. The Socratic payload bank and the AIVSS CWE lookup table are fixtures, not live intelligence.
    3. Legacy shells. The interactive hub, the curses TUI, and the setup wizard predate the scriptable CLI and are kept for internal experimentation only.
    4. Environment coupling. The sandbox probe runner needs a local container runtime, and the Sigstore attestation path needs the optional sigstore extra.

    When a Tier 4 helper proves broadly useful, the correct move is to promote a narrow, well-tested slice of it into Tier 1 or Tier 2 — never to re-label the harness itself as a customer feature.

    The two guard-related entries and Wave 4 benchmark decoupling deserve a specific note:

    • Internal Benchmark & Regression Verification Infrastructure (Pro). The 32-server VAIB Docker lab, the 51 DB scenario oracles, and ControlledPenetrationEngine are Internal Benchmark & Regression Verification Infrastructure used for CI and engineering QA — not the primary customer operating mode.
    • Fail-closed Pro verification & rollback. DatabaseStateVerifier.verify_mongodb_cross_tenant_isolation(allow_synthetic_fallback=False) fails closed when MongoDB is unreachable instead of reporting synthetic fixture passes (emitting a [SYNTHETIC]-prefixed reason only when allow_synthetic_fallback=True), and ControlledPenetrationEngine executes attack and rollback inside try/finally so rollback_confirmed is True only after rollback and post-rollback baseline verification succeed (details["rollback_error"] is recorded on failure).
    • Configurable tenancy & schema-driven plans (Pro). PolicyGatekeeper(allowed_tenants=()) defaults to an empty allowlist so production targets are not rejected for lacking synthetic lab tenant names, while get_deterministic_plan() synthesizes neutral schema-driven proposals from McpServerSpec tool schemas for unregistered servers instead of falling back to CUSTOMER_CRUD_PLAN.
    • Hardened ReadOnlySafetyGuard & decoupled DualPathRouter (Free). ReadOnlySafetyGuard extends destructive-verb coverage to block run_sql, execute_sql, upsert, post_, put_, patch_, chmod, and kill (using boundary matching for post_, put_, and patch_), while DualPathRouter removes the hardcoded "formula": "1 + 1" payload in favor of a neutral schema-driven required-argument skeleton (surfacing NEEDS_ARGS when a required parameter cannot be synthesized safely and preserving caller-supplied arguments verbatim). Real SQL deployments should still use SqlSecurityGuard, and the hardcoded f-string guard templates are honest only when described as a Guard Snippet Generator.

    Scope discipline¶

    Tier 3 and Tier 4 exist so the primary docs stay honest. When a new capability is added to the code base, the first question is not "does it work?" but "which tier does it belong to?". A capability that only proves the engine works belongs in Tier 4. A capability that a customer uses occasionally belongs in Tier 3. Only capabilities a customer depends on day to day belong in Tier 1 or Tier 2.

    This discipline keeps three promises:

    1. A small, honest primary story. Customers see only what they need.
    2. A complete, auditable inventory. Nothing disappears from the docs.
    3. A stable extension path. Power users can still reach the extras.

    Frequently asked questions¶

    Is a Tier 3 utility unsupported? No. Tier 3 utilities are supported and documented; they are simply not part of the primary product story.

    Can I depend on a Tier 4 harness in CI? Only inside the Verity QA pipeline. Tier 4 harnesses are pinned to internal fixtures and may change without a customer-facing deprecation cycle.

    Why keep anything at score 1.70? Because removing working code costs more than documenting it honestly. The tool collision resolver stays for reproducibility of historical scans.

    Where do new capabilities land? New customer capabilities land in Tier 1 or Tier 2. Anything that only proves the engine works lands in Tier 4 and never appears on the homepage.
